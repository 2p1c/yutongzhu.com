In fact, everything comes down to how you think, and people usually know exactly what they should do. All we truly need is focus, courage, and devotion. Everything you need to know is available online—you really don’t need to hire a personal trainer (in most cases), nor do you need to see a doctor expecting a pill that will make you stronger (I’m talking about situations where there’s nothing wrong with your body and you just want to become healthier). It’s like knowing you need to sleep early and wake up early, avoid looking at your phone before bed, get some exercise and stretching, drink more water, and eat cleaner food. But you know how it is—people always choose the easier path over the right one. They keep avoiding, drifting downstream along the deepest groove of inertia in their minds. Many times, you don’t genuinely want to achieve something—good grades, attention, or that job opportunity. You’re just afraid of being left behind, afraid of not fitting in, afraid of society’s judgment. Fear-driven motivation only leads to a vicious cycle, slowly draining your creativity, drive, and sense of purpose until you no longer know what you truly want.

The solution sounds simple on the surface: ‘Find what you genuinely want to do,’ to possess both poetry and bread. But who the hell knows? Often, it feels like the life meant for you is happening somewhere else, and only when you truly arrive there will you stop wrestling with this question. When you close your eyes, when raindrops fall, when you look at yourself in the mirror—we all catch a faint glimpse of a path, a possibility. The question then circles back to how much you truly want it. How much do you want that great physique? Enough to swallow every meal like a bull, minding calories and sugar, showing up at the gym no matter what, forcing yourself to sleep, adjusting your body through sunlight, analyzing your condition, enduring the pain and exhausted nerves, then pulling yourself back together. Push yourself to the limit again and again, connecting mind and muscle, until one day you catch yourself in the mirror and realize you’ve changed—but this is still far from enough.
