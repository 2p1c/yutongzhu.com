Actually, everything depends on how you think, and in fact, people often know exactly what they should do. The internet has all the knowledge you need—you really don't need to find a personal trainer (in most cases), nor do you need to see a doctor hoping they'll prescribe a pill that cures you (I mean when there's nothing wrong with your body and you just want to become healthier). It's like you know you need to go to bed early and wake up early, avoid looking at your phone before sleep, do some exercise and stretching, drink more water, and eat clean food. But you know, people always do the easier thing rather than the right thing. People always avoid, yielding to the deepest groove of inertia in their brains. Many times, you don't really want to do something—get good grades, be noticed by others, get that job opportunity—you're just afraid of being left behind, afraid of not fitting in, afraid of society's evaluation system. Fear-driven behavior only leads to a vicious cycle, and people gradually lose creativity, motivation, goals, and even the knowledge of what they truly want.

The solution to all this is simple to say—'find what you truly want to do'—but who the hell knows? Maybe once you find it, you won't need to think about this question anymore. Some people always know. When closing their eyes, when raindrops fall, when looking into the mirror—we all know what we should do. The question comes back to how much you really want it. How much do you want to build a great physique, swallow all the food like a bull, show up at the gym no matter what, force yourself to sleep, and in the morning feel the sunlight, the pain, and the frazzled nerves, then pull yourself together, pushing yourself to the limit again and again, connecting your mind and muscles—one more, one more, and one more.
